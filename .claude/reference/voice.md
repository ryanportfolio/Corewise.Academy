# House voice: CoreWise Academy site and guide prose

> The voice for everything a visitor reads on corewise.academy. The global `writing`
> skill loads this file on top of its editorial rules; where they conflict, this file
> wins (the em-dash ban always holds). The *rules* (bans, gates, rulings with dates)
> stay canonical in `copy-rules.md`; this file is the sound.

Scope: site copy and guides only. Ryan's personal voice for applications, LinkedIn,
and outreach lives in the githelp repo (`reference/voice.md` there); do not mix the
two. That voice is blunt and fragmentary; this one is the essay voice below.

## The model

The plain declarative essay. Morgan Housel's Collab Fund posts are the reference:
"'Smart' is the ability to solve problems." "That was life." Make the claim, then
earn it with a concrete example. No throat-clearing before the claim, no reframing
after it.

## Fingerprint

- **Typing-voice, not blog-voice.** Plain sentences a sharp person would type to a
  smart friend, not polished blog cadence.
- **Rhythm:** short sentences carry the point; long ones carry the evidence. Vary
  them. A little asymmetry is welcome; perfect parallel structure reads generated.
- **First person where genuine.** "I" is fine when the editor actually did or
  believes the thing. Never as a persona.
- **Honest edges.** Say plainly what is uncertain, unproven, or a matter of taste.
  Mixed findings beat one-note confidence.
- **Reference mode, not teaching mode.** Guides state the claim, give at most one
  illustration, move on. No ramps, no reassurance, no hand-holding.
- **Concrete over conceptual.** Name the thing before any framing: "We use a skill
  called caveman for this", never "The contract we actually run is...". Numbers,
  file names, and real examples beat adjectives.
- **Stakes live inside the prose** (the afternoon lost, the tokens burned), never
  under a "why this matters" label.

## What this voice is not

- Not corporate or promotional. No feature-tour cadence, no "seamless", no selling.
- Not academic. No hedged passive constructions, no citations-as-armor.
- Not clever-first. Wordplay only on top of a plain, specific point, never instead
  of one.
- Not the outreach voice. No "at the end of the day", no arrow chains, no spoken
  run-ons. Those belong to githelp drafts in Ryan's name.

## Where the rules live

Every ban and gate (em dashes, heading periods, negation pivots, jargon, walls of
text, scaffolding cuts) is in `copy-rules.md` with its ruling date. Procedures:
`/plain-words` (fancy-word sweep), `/stranger-test` (insider-frame fix),
`/humanizer` (AI-tell catalog). This file only records how the site sounds when
those rules are already obeyed.
