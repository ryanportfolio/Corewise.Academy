## Source checks

- **Karpathy's post**, https://x.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046, October 11, 2026, read in full through the twitter-search script (twitterapi.io). The four group sizes (about 6B / 75%; 1-2B / about 20%; about 20M / 0.2%; about 5,000) and the three factors are quoted from it. He calls the numbers "napkin math"; the guide says they are his rough estimates. His explanation for factor 2: "LLM capability emerges from reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards on a curated environment mixture driven by revenue potential." The guide paraphrases that in plain words.
- **Agentic coding and persistent returns to expertise**, Anthropic, June 16, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise. Novice sessions: "about five Claude actions and roughly 600 words of output" per prompt. Expert sessions: "action chains more than twice as long (12 actions)" and "five times the output (3,200 words)". Expertise is Claude's five-point rating of the user's apparent expertise at the task. "domain expertise, and not coding proficiency, amplifies effective use of the tool." The video's wording ("twice as long with five times the output") matches. The guide adds the definition of expertise and that the study is observational.
- **How employment is changing at Anthropic**, October 16, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic. "Coding-focused AI agent sessions for non-technical employees (identified as those with no code commits at baseline, excluding new hires) leapt from 2 per month in August 2025 to 90 in May 2026." Neither the page nor the PDF (page 4) says whether this is per person or a total. The guide gives the ratio between the two figures, 45-fold, which holds for either unit. The video says staff "will have 2 to 90 coding agent sessions a month", which reads as a range; the source gives a rise over nine months. The sales representative's dashboard ("three to four hours") is from the same page.
- Not verified: the video's claim that Opus 5.5 on medium effort is the current default recommendation in Claude. The guide says to stay on the app's default model and names no model.
- "Running it uses little or none of your limit": the video says code "causes zero tokens to run". Claude starting a program and reading its output still uses some, so the guide softens the claim.
